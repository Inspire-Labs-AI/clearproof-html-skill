## STE has two parts: writing rules and a controlled dictionary {kicker="What it is"}
ASD-STE100 is a controlled language: a small, strict subset of English. ASD maintains it. ASD is the AeroSpace and Defence Industries Association of Europe. The current issue is Issue 9, from 15 January 2025.
```kv
Writing rules | 53 rules, in two groups: procedures and descriptive text
Dictionary | About 900 approved words. Each has one meaning and one part of speech.
Also in dictionary | Words you must not use, with an approved word to use instead
Maintained by | ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group (STEMG)
Current issue | Issue 9, 15 January 2025
Made for | Aircraft maintenance manuals, read by non-native English speakers
```
The dictionary is the less known half. The dictionary approves a word like "close" for one meaning only. The writer cannot use it for another meaning.

## It began in 1983 as an airline request for manuals people could not misread {kicker="Origin"}
European airlines asked for a standard English for maintenance manuals. Many technicians did not read English as a first language. A misread step on an aircraft can cause damage or injury.
```timeline
1983 | AECMA Simplified English work starts | At the request of European airlines (AEA)
1986 | First guide published | AECMA document PSC-85-16598
2004 | AECMA merges into ASD | The specification takes the name ASD-STE100
2025 | Issue 9 published | 15 January 2025. It also moves from a specification toward an international standard.
```
The group has made 17 releases in all, counting full issues, changes and revisions.

## The rules give hard numbers: 20 words per step, 25 per description, 6 sentences per paragraph {kicker="Rules"}
```chart bar unit=" words" caption="Sentence-length limits in STE. Paragraphs also have a limit: 6 sentences."
Procedure sentence | 20
*Descriptive sentence | 25 ! one topic per paragraph
```
| Rule | What it says | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Procedure sentence length | At most 20 words | Stop the engine. |
| Description sentence length | At most 25 words | The valve keeps the pressure in the tank below the limit. |
| Paragraph length | At most 6 sentences, one topic | - |
| One idea per sentence | One instruction per step sentence | Close the door. (not: close the door and test the lock) |
| Commands for steps | Use the imperative | Remove the cover. |
| Active voice | Say who does the action | The pump moves the fuel. |
| Approved words only | Use dictionary words in their approved meaning | - |
| One word, one meaning | Do not use one word for two meanings | - |
| Short noun groups | Avoid long stacks of nouns; the rules limit them | - |
| Same term every time | Do not swap synonyms for variety | - |
Several secondary sources confirm the first three rows. Rows after that are from memory of the standard and from summaries. The exact rule numbers are not given here.

## Rewrite 1: a verbose answer becomes 5 short steps {kicker="Before and after"}
The question: "Why does my server say the port is in use, and how do I fix it?"
| Typical LLM answer | Same answer in STE |
|---|---|
| Great question! The "port already in use" error that you are seeing is generally caused by the fact that another process, which may possibly be an earlier instance of your own server that was never properly shut down, is still holding on to the port that your application is attempting to bind to, and it is typically a good idea to first try to identify which process it might be before you decide to do anything drastic. In most cases, you will probably want to begin by running a command such as lsof, which should hopefully show you the process that is occupying the port, and then, assuming that the process is not something important, it may be worth considering terminating it so that the port can potentially be freed up. It is also worth noting that, depending on your operating system and setup, the exact commands might vary a little, and in some situations you might also want to simply choose a different port altogether, which could be an easier solution. | The error means that another process uses the port. Often this process is an old copy of your server. Do these steps: (1) Find the process. Run: lsof -i :3000. (2) Read the PID in the second column. (3) Make sure that the process is not important. (4) Stop the process. Run: kill PID. (5) Start your server again. If you cannot stop the process, use a different port. The commands are for Linux and macOS. Windows uses different commands. |
What changed: the verbose version hides the command inside a 54-word sentence, uses passive forms and hedges ("possibly", "hopefully", "potentially"). The STE version states the cause once, then gives one action per line.

## Rewrite 2: a rambling prompt becomes a labelled task {kicker="Before and after"}
| Typical prompt | Same prompt in STE style |
|---|---|
| You are a world-class senior software engineer and an extremely helpful, friendly assistant with decades of experience. I was kind of wondering if you could maybe help me out with something, because I have been having some issues with my app lately and I am not really sure what is going on. Basically, whenever I try to start it up it sometimes crashes with some kind of error about a port, and I think it might possibly be related to something I changed recently but I honestly can't remember what it was. Please be thorough but also try not to be too long, and make it good if you can, thanks so much in advance! | Task: Find the cause of a startup error. Give the fix. Context: A Node.js server crashes at start. The error says that the port is in use. I cannot say when the error started. Rules: Write steps as commands. Use no more than 20 words in each step. Give a maximum of 6 steps. Output: First, give the most probable cause in one sentence. Then give the steps. |
The old prompt has a persona line, vague words ("some kind of", "thorough but not too long") and no stated output. The new prompt names the task, the facts, the limits and the output. Note that it adds facts the old prompt left out: the language and the exact error.

## A script measured both rewrites: no sentence over 10 words, no hedges {kicker="Proof"}
The script splits text into sentences and counts words. It also counts hedge words such as "maybe", "possibly", "might" and "hopefully". The "hedges" column is that count.
```run
$ node examples/ste100/measure.mjs examples/ste100
shows: ans_after      hedges=0 words=75 sentences=13 avg=5.8 longest=10 over25=0
note: Four lines: prompt_before, prompt_after, ans_before, ans_after. Sentence splitting is simple, so treat the counts as approximate.
```

## STE differs from common prompting in six ways {kicker="Comparison"}
| Common practice today | STE practice | Why STE may help |
|---|---|---|
| Long free-form prompt | Labelled parts: task, context, rules, output | The reader finds each fact fast |
| Persona text ("world-class expert") | Facts and limits | A persona adds no facts |
| Hedged, long answers | One claim per sentence, no filler | You can verify each sentence |
| Ambiguous words ("it", "make it good") | One word, one meaning | Fewer wrong readings |
| Steps in prose | Steps as commands, 20 words at most | You can follow while working |
| Idioms and phrasal verbs | Plain verbs | Non-native readers and translators cope |
This table is partly inference. See the limits below.

## What is verified, what is inferred, what is not known {kicker="Honest limits"}
```claims
verified | STE has 53 writing rules and about 900 approved dictionary words | https://www.tcworld.info/news/asd-ste100-issue-9-setting-a-standard-for-technical-documentation
verified | Procedure sentences: 20 words at most. Description sentences: 25 words at most. Paragraphs: 6 sentences at most. | https://skybrary.aero/articles/simplified-technical-english-ste
inferred | Karpathy suggested asking an LLM for ASD-STE100 output, sometimes only 80% of the way | reported by https://www.searchenginejournal.com/karpathy-llm-aircraft-manual-writing/591813/
inferred | STE-style output is easier to verify, because each sentence makes one claim | basis: the one-idea rule, and our measured example
inferred | Non-native readers and machine translation cope better with STE-style model output | basis: STE was made for non-native readers; no LLM-specific study found
unverified | STE-style prompts make a model answer more correctly | no controlled study found; one community skill reports fewer STE-rule violations, which measures style, not correctness
unverified | Karpathy's exact words and date | we could not open the original post; we use reports of it
```
Real limits also exist. Its authors made STE for maintenance steps, not for argument, poetry or fine nuance. A 900-word dictionary cannot name every idea, and the writer must add technical names. Strict rules can flatten a careful "it depends" into a false certainty. Karpathy himself reportedly asks for about 80% of the standard, because the full standard is strict.

## Karpathy's ladder goes from STE text to diagrams, HTML and video {kicker="Context"}
```flow LR
STE text -> Diagram: easier than text
Diagram -> Interactive HTML: animation, controls
Interactive HTML -> Explainer video: 3b1b style, narrated
```
Reports say he ranks each step "even better" than the one before. STE is the first and cheapest step. This page itself is the third step.

## Check yourself {kicker="Quiz"}
```quiz
? A step reads: "The filter should be removed and the cover then closed." What does STE ask for?
- [x] Two commands: "Remove the filter. Close the cover." :: Active voice, imperative, one instruction per sentence.
- [ ] Keep it. It is under 20 words. :: Length is not the only rule; passive voice and two actions break others.
- [ ] Add "please" and "carefully". :: Filler words add no information.
? Which sentence limit applies to a descriptive paragraph?
- [x] 25 words per sentence, 6 sentences per paragraph :: These are the descriptive limits.
- [ ] 20 words per sentence, 3 sentences per paragraph :: 20 is the procedure limit; 3 is not a stated paragraph limit.
- [ ] There is no limit :: The rules set hard numbers.
? Does this page prove that STE prompts give more correct answers?
- [ ] Yes, the measured numbers show it :: They show shorter sentences, not correctness.
- [x] No. It shows style changes only. :: Correctness is unverified.
```
